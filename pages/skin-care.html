Skin care

Cleanse gently, moisturise and protect exposed skin from the sun.

On this page

Your daily routine

Morning

  1. Cleanse Rinse with water or use a gentle cleanser.
  2. Moisturise Apply to slightly damp skin.
  3. Protect Apply sunscreen to exposed skin before going outdoors.

Evening

  1. Cleanse Wash off sunscreen, sweat and makeup with a gentle cleanser.
  2. Moisturise Pat dry and apply while your skin is still slightly damp.

What to look for on the label

Sunscreen

Look for broad-spectrum, water-resistant SPF 30 or higher for protection against UVA and UVB. If sunscreen stings, try a fragrance-free mineral formula with zinc oxide, titanium dioxide or both.

Watch for fragrance if your skin reacts to it.

Cover exposed skin, including your ears and neck. Reapply about every two hours outdoors and after swimming or sweating. Use shade and protective clothing when you can.

Moisturiser

Look for a plain, fragrance-free moisturiser. Glycerin and ceramides are useful ingredients. A petrolatum-based ointment can help very dry patches. If you’re prone to breakouts, look for non-comedogenic on the label.

Watch for fragrance or parfum if you have sensitive skin. “Unscented” products can still contain ingredients that mask odour. Check for “fragrance-free” on the label.

Gentle cleanser

Look for a gentle, fragrance-free, soap-free cleanser. Use your fingertips and lukewarm water, then pat dry. Cleanse after heavy sweating.

Watch for scrub particles, strongly fragranced washes and cleansers that leave your skin tight or stinging. Choose a milder wash if that happens.

Singapore cosmetic safety warnings

  • Skip unlabelled creams and miracle claims. Buy from established retailers. Check for a full ingredient list and batch number. Treat “HSA approved” claims on cosmetics or promises of whitening in days as red flags.
  • Check HSA alerts before using a suspect product. HSA has found undeclared mercury, hydroquinone, tretinoin and potent steroids in some cosmetic creams. Mercury is prohibited as a cosmetic ingredient. Hydroquinone and tretinoin have legitimate prescription uses under medical supervision.
  • Test one new product at a time. Try it on a small area first, following the label’s rinse-off instructions. If you develop a rash, stop using the product. Seek medical advice if the reaction is severe or persists.
