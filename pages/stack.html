Food

Weekday meals

Build meals around vegetables, protein and modest portions of lower-GI carbs such as beans and lentils. Make plain water your everyday drink.

Overnight oats

  • Start with plain rolled oats. Try ⅓ cup dry oats with chia seeds and a handful of berries. Choose plain oats over sweetened sachets.
  • Soak the chia seeds overnight. Stir the oats and seeds into plain milk or unsweetened fortified soy milk and refrigerate. Add a spoonful of peanut butter without added sugar.
  • Try a smaller oat portion if needed. Keep the berries and chia and use plain, unsweetened yoghurt with live cultures if you enjoy and tolerate it.

Hotpot

  • Start with greens and mushrooms. Try chye sim, kai lan, bok choy or broccoli.
  • Choose fresh fish, lean meat or firm tofu. Have fishballs, luncheon meat and fatty slices less often.
  • Use edamame as your side. Add a small scoop of shelled edamame in place of rice or noodles.
  • Leave most of the broth in the pot. Use less sauce and choose a lower-sodium soup base when available.

Quick meals for lunch or dinner

Chickpea and cucumber salad

  1. Drain and rinse ½ cup of canned chickpeas. Roughly mash with lemon juice, a little olive oil and black pepper.
  2. Toss with cucumber, tomato and leafy greens. Add an egg or some firm tofu if you want a more substantial lunch.

Why choose it · Chickpeas are a lower-GI source of carbs and fibre.

Salmon, lentils and greens

  1. Cook a salmon fillet with lemon and pepper. Warm ½ cup of cooked lentils and steam frozen broccoli or fresh chye sim.
  2. For a pantry option, use lower-sodium canned sardines.

Why choose it · Oily fish provides omega-3 fats. Lentils add fibre and replace the usual rice.

Build meals for steadier glucose

  • Swap the staple. Choose chickpeas, lentils or edamame in place of your usual rice, bread or noodles. Add plenty of non-starchy vegetables and a protein such as fish, eggs or tofu.
  • Check the portion as well as the GI. GI compares how carbohydrate foods raise blood glucose. How much carbohydrate you eat also matters. If you choose rice or bread, try a smaller portion alongside vegetables and protein. Choose whole fruit over juice.
  • Review HbA1c over time. It reflects roughly three months of average blood glucose. Trials of lower-GI or lower-glycaemic-load eating patterns found modest average HbA1c improvements in people with diabetes. Ask your care team what target and retest timing fit you.

If you use insulin or a sulfonylurea such as gliclazide, check with your care team before substantially cutting carbs because of the risk of low blood glucose.

What helps over time

Rotate fibre-rich foods

Alternate oats, chickpeas, lentils and different fruits and vegetables across the week. Fibre and enough fluid support regular bowel movements. Build up gradually if you usually eat little fibre.

Fermented foods are optional. In a small trial in healthy adults, a diet higher in a variety of fermented foods increased gut microbe diversity.

Drink water regularly

Start with a glass of plain water at meals and keep a refillable bottle nearby between meals. Drink more when you sweat in the heat or exercise. Follow your care team’s advice if you have a fluid limit.

Repeat a Mediterranean-style pattern

Build most meals around vegetables, fruit, beans and whole grains. Use unsaturated oils and include fish. A three-year Mediterranean diet study in adults at higher cardiovascular risk found improvements in some blood markers of inflammation.

Keep your meals safe

  1. Clean and separate Wash hands with soap. Keep raw meat and seafood apart from cooked food and salad. Use separate boards or wash them thoroughly between tasks. At hotpot, use separate utensils for raw and cooked food.
  2. Cook through Cook meat and seafood thoroughly. If batch cooking, put the portions you plan to save into clean, covered containers and refrigerate promptly at 4°C or below. Discard cooked food left at room temperature for more than two hours.
  3. Reheat once Heat leftovers to at least 75°C for two minutes. Reheat only the portion you’ll eat, in a container rated for the appliance you’re using.
