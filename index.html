High-impact priorities

Stay smoke-free and drink less

If you smoke, get help to quit. Avoid second-hand smoke. For cancer prevention, choosing alcohol-free drinks reduces exposure to a known cause of cancer.

Make everyday meals count

Build meals around vegetables, fruit, whole grains and beans. Choose portions that help you maintain a healthy weight. At hotpot, favour fish or tofu, choose less processed meat and leave most of the broth.

Apple cider vinegar and diabetes prevention

Small studies have tested whether vinegar affects blood sugar after a meal. They don’t establish that apple cider vinegar prevents type 2 diabetes.

If you enjoy it, use a little as a dressing. Avoid drinking it straight because the acid can harm tooth enamel and irritate your throat. Ask a pharmacist before using it regularly as a supplement if you take insulin or a diuretic.

For diabetes prevention, build meals around vegetables and fibre-rich foods, and make regular activity part of your week.

Build fitness and strength

Cardio for busy days and older beginners

VO₂ max is a measure of aerobic fitness. To build that fitness, choose a pace that makes you breathe faster while you can still talk in sentences. Use how you feel to set the pace.

  1. Start with 5–10 minutes. Try a flat walk after lunch or an easy ride on a stationary bike if that’s more comfortable. Start with a few minutes if needed.
  2. Fit it around your day. Build toward 150 minutes of moderate activity a week. Three 10-minute brisk walks on each weekday add up to 150 minutes.
  3. Add short brisk intervals. Once regular walks feel comfortable, alternate one brisk minute with two easy minutes. Keep the brisk intervals at a pace where you can still talk. Over time, notice whether the same route feels easier.

Stop and seek medical advice if exercise causes chest discomfort, dizziness or unusual breathlessness. In Singapore, call 995 for severe chest pain, severe trouble breathing or loss of consciousness.

Simple bodyweight strength at home

Start with one round on two days a week, with a rest day between them. Use a sturdy chair that won’t slide and a stable wall or counter for support.

  • Chair sit-to-stand · 5 reps. Stand up from the chair, then sit back down slowly. Use your hands for support if needed.
  • Wall push-up · 5 reps. Place your palms on the wall at chest height. Bend your elbows to lean in, then push away.
  • Supported heel raise · 5 reps. Hold a counter, rise onto your toes and lower slowly.

Add a few reps or a second round when the first round feels comfortable. If you’re 65 or older, aim to practise strength and balance together on at least three days a week. For balance, try a few heel-to-toe steps alongside a wall you can touch for support.

Sleep better and manage insomnia

What to do tonight

  1. Pick a steady wake time. Get up around the same time each day, even after a rough night.
  2. Give yourself time to wind down. Avoid caffeine for at least four hours before bed. Dim the lights and choose a quiet activity. Go to bed when you feel sleepy.
  3. If you’re wide awake in bed, reset. If you can get up safely, do something quiet in dim light and return to bed when you feel sleepy again.
HealthHub · Better sleep habits

When sleep problems keep coming back

Cognitive behavioural therapy for insomnia (CBT-I) is the first-line treatment for chronic insomnia. If trouble falling or staying asleep keeps recurring or affects your day, book a GP or polyclinic visit and ask about CBT-I.

Bring a short sleep log with your bedtimes, awakenings and wake times. Mention loud snoring, waking up gasping or marked daytime sleepiness so your clinician can check for sleep apnoea.

Illness prevention and recovery

Reduce your chance of respiratory illness

  • Check which respiratory vaccines are due, including flu and COVID-19 if recommended for you.
  • Wash your hands regularly and choose outdoor or well-ventilated spaces when possible. Consider a well-fitting mask in crowded indoor spaces, especially around people at higher risk.
Adult vaccination guide Respiratory virus prevention

Recover when you’re unwell

  • Rest at home, drink fluids regularly within any limits your clinician has set, and wear a mask around others. Pause strenuous exercise while ill and ease back in once you feel better.
  • Contact your clinic if symptoms persist or worsen, or early if you’re at higher risk of severe illness. Call 995 for severe breathing difficulty or severe chest pain in Singapore.

Sauna and cold plunges

Optional recovery practices. Prioritise regular movement, strength training and sleep.

Sauna

A Finnish cohort linked frequent sauna use with lower mortality. The study observed people’s habits, so the effect of sauna use on lifespan remains uncertain.

If you use one · Stay hydrated, avoid alcohol, cool down gradually and leave if you feel dizzy or unwell. Ask your clinician first if you have heart disease or blood pressure problems.

Finnish sauna cohort Cleveland Clinic · Sauna safety

Cold plunge

Cold-water immersion may ease short-term soreness after hard exercise. Frequent plunges immediately after lifting may blunt muscle growth. Effects on lifespan remain uncertain.

If you use one · Cold shock can abruptly increase breathing, heart rate and blood pressure. Avoid plunging alone or jumping straight in after a sauna. Ask your clinician first if you have heart or circulation problems.
